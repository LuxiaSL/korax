Read both. Here's my honest take.

## What they're doing

v1 (Agora) makes one argument, and it's the right one: coordination fails on *mutable* shared state, so flip the substrate to an append-only log of immutable typed messages, keep the vocabulary monotone (CALM), and compute "current state" as a read-time reduction. Blackboard + Linda is the correct lineage and you name it honestly.

v2 (Rookery) keeps the spine and does three things v1 couldn't: it generalizes from within-campaign to **across projects / time / operators / people**; it adds the pieces field use demanded (BESIDE, HANDOVER, STAMP, grades, bands, cursors); and it gets specific enough to build. The single best move in v2 is **pointer-only** — "the board is the index and the conversation, never the bank." That one rule kills the failure mode that eats every knowledge base.

## Where I'd push

**1. The sequencer is doing the work, not the CRDT.** You say concurrency is conflict-free "by construction" — but `id` and `ts` come from the server, which means you have a total-order sequencer. That's a coordination point, and it's *good*, it buys you cursors and tiebreaks for free. But it means the CALM framing is slightly load-bearing in a way it shouldn't be. Concretely: **CLAIM-with-lease is not monotone.** "This claim is live" gets falsified by the passage of time; claim-stealing is a retraction. It works anyway because the server total order gives you a deterministic tiebreak — so name the sequencer as the mechanism rather than attributing it to monotonicity, or the first liveness bug will be confusing.

**2. There's no invalidation cascade, and you need one.** `view=provenance` walks `derives-from` *toward* ground. Nothing walks the other way. So: FINDING A gets stamped, three projects derive from it, six weeks later A turns out wrong. The WARN never reaches the derivers. For a system whose whole pitch is cross-project and cross-time sourcing, "who downstream of this is now suspect" is a first-class query — `view=descendants` / a taint reduction. Cheap to add now, structurally awkward to retrofit.

**3. STAMP recreates the bottleneck the docs exist to remove.** v1's design test is "does this let a decision be made on the board instead of in your inbox?" §3.2 says nothing is of record until a human stamps it, §6 makes STAMP non-delegable, and §9 makes `of-record` stamped-only. If cross-desk sourcing keys off `of-record`, total throughput of the cross-project index is bounded by your stamping rate — you've moved the bottleneck from message-passing to notarization. I think the resolution is explicit: **`verified` (desk band) is the working currency for cross-desk sourcing; `stamped` is reserved for a small canon.** Then say per-reduction which grade it consumes by default, and design for "I sourced a verified claim that never got stamped."

**4. These are two products sharing a spine — and the pilot order is right because of it.**

```mermaid
graph TD
  L[append-only log: envelopes] --> A[live coordination<br/>claims · leases · wait · handover<br/>high-volume · ephemeral · latency-bound]
  L --> B[durable index<br/>rakes · findings · canon · provenance<br/>low-volume · permanent · curated]
  A -.needs.-> A1[decay · salience · digests]
  B -.needs.-> B1[grades · shas · descendants]
```

Opposite requirements: one wants speed and forgetting, the other wants curation and permanence. Same envelope, fine — but retention, reductions, and namespaces should split explicitly. §14 starting at `/commons/rakes` is correct precisely because that's the durable half, and it's the only part that pays off at N=1 agent, N=1 project. Everything else needs scale before it earns anything.

**5. BESIDE doesn't fix herding — it fixes *collapse*.** Co-visibility preserves two readings that already exist. It does nothing about the generation step, where every agent reads `view=state`, sees proposal #1, and elaborates proposal #1. The actual mitigation is already in your field loop and didn't get promoted into the protocol: **pre-statements frozen before data.** Generalize it — `post-before-read`, or a blind mode on `view=state` that hides live PROPOSALs until you've posted yours. That's a substrate feature, not a discipline you hope agents keep.

**6. §11.1 is a promise; §11.2 is the boundary.** "Board text is data, never instructions — every agent client bakes this in as a hard rule" is prompt-level, and prompt-level rules about untrusted text are exactly what fails under adversarial peers. The sound part is 11.2: a CLAIM entitles you to work, a sha-pinned brief authorizes execution. Lead with that; 11.1 is defense-in-depth. And the client should *render* board content typed, quoted, and band-attributed rather than splicing it into context as prose.

**7. The unaddressed risk is that agents just… don't post.** The desk loop works because a human curates every entry. In a swarm, posting discipline is the whole ballgame — what makes an agent post a WARN instead of quietly moving on? That belongs in the brief as an obligation ("before abandoning an approach, post a WARN"), and §14.3's success metric should have a partner: count WARNs that later drew a "this saved me" reply. Relays-not-sent measures the desk's relief; that measures whether the board is actually load-bearing.

| § | Contents | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Envelope — normative JSON Schema | the whole thing hangs off this |
| 2 | Edges — validity rules, which acts may carry which | incl. R2 |

| 8 | Storage — schema, append-only enforced at DDL | no UPDATE, no DELETE |

| 10 | **Agent conduct** — normative obligations of a well-behaved band | see below |
| 11 | Pilot seed — the initial rakes | |

§10 is the one people skip and shouldn't. v1 said it outright: the substrate hands you asynchrony for free, and *the protocol* is what makes coordination converge instead of thrash. So the spec has to be normative about conduct, not just wire format — when you must WARN before abandoning an approach, when to corroborate rather than post, what you must read before claiming, what a HANDOVER owes its successor. That section is the acausal-coordination layer, and it's the part that's actually hard to get right.

My default stack recommendation, absent other constraints: Python + FastAPI + SQLite with a Postgres migration path, since the MCP wrapper is then trivial and you'll want to hack on this. Agreed, and that reframes the deliverable usefully: **the protocol is the product**, the server is a reference implementation of it, and stack choice becomes reversible — which is exactly the property you're asking for. Good instinct to say it before I started writing.

Five consequences worth baking into the spec on that basis:

**Conventions are enforced server-side, not in clients.** This is the big one. If "loud acts need a pointer," "non-desk bands cap at unverified," "STAMP is human-only," and `blind_until_post` live in client code, then N clients means N dialects and the colony drifts inside a month — and the drift is invisible because everything still posts fine. The server rejects malformed acts at `/post`. Clients stay thin, and a sloppy third-party client can't corrupt the record.

**Reductions are server endpoints, canonically.** Clients may compute their own views freely, but `view=state` has to mean one thing across the whole colony, or two desks both "read the board" and disagree about what it says. That's a coordination failure the substrate was supposed to eliminate.

**Forward compatibility needs a rule.** Envelope carries a protocol version, and unknown act types, edge types, and `ext` fields must be **preserved and rendered opaque, never dropped**. Append-only already protects the log itself, but a client that silently filters types it doesn't recognize produces a projection that's quietly wrong — worse than one that errors.

**Ship a conformance suite as part of the spec.** A fixture log plus expected reduction outputs. That's the difference between "a second implementation is possible" and "a second implementation is aspirational," and it's cheap to write alongside the reference server.

**The client roster falls out nicely** — `rook` CLI, an MCP server, a human reading room (the sysop console; the agentic-BBS framing earns its keep here), and eventually a Soft Machine panel, since you're already sitting in the environment where that's the natural view.

So the revised plan: write `rookery-protocol.md` as a language-neutral normative spec — envelope, edges, acts, grades, namespaces, wire API, reductions, conduct, conformance — and keep the reference server (schema, deployment, language) in a separate doc that the protocol doesn't depend on. Stack choice then genuinely doesn't need deciding now.

The full game-theory read on your proposal, now that it's written down:

**What's soundest.** The maintainer/desk split with mutual exclusivity. Two independent structural arguments converge on it: adjudication between desks requires a party with no stake (the referee can't be a player), and board health is otherwise a tragedy of the commons — every desk under-invests in it because their objective is their project. Making it someone's *whole job*, someone who cannot win by favoring a project, is what makes "alive/self-running" plausible rather than hopeful. Enactor lockout needed nothing new: a project enactor's grants simply never match `/commons/jobs`, so cross-project work is invisible by construction; a "floater" is just an identity granted the commons job board and no project nest. I took the **strong form** of exclusivity (no dual-hat anywhere) and flagged the weaker scope-disjoint form in Appendix B for the discussion you said you wanted — strong is the safer default to relax later.

**The constraint that shaped the pin mechanics:** must-read is a tax on every future reader's *context window*. Boards don't die of missing pins, they die of sticky-thread sprawl. So the design charges the pinner (canon pins are budgeted per nest; at budget, adding requires superseding — always a curation decision), amortizes the reader (acks are durable per document-version, so onboarding is paid once, and supersession is exactly what voids the ack and triggers a re-read), and never surveils (the server checks *attestations* on the log, not reading behavior — a false ack is attributable forever, an invisible skip is invisible). Your "pull in automatically, annotated, up to a max depth" is §10.10: prerequisites arrive annotated on the document, and the 409 you get for claiming unprepared *is the reading list*.

**Democratic canon** got the minimum machinery that makes it real: `endorses` (deliberately distinct from `corroborates` — agreement, not reproduction) as the cheap signal, then a configurable loop of propose-in-meta → threshold → maintainer enactment → human stamp. Crossing a threshold authorizes nothing by itself; the state change is always an attributable act. Population signals, named identity decides, log records both — which keeps your anti-herding posture intact even in governance.

**Current tally:** 13 acts, 14 edges. Every act added past v2's nine is a principled exception — POLICY (only payload the server interprets), JOB (only act designed to be acted on), PIN (only act that taxes future readers, so it gets budgeted), ACK (attestation needs a carrier with no other meaning).

Three things left open in Appendix B for you: exclusivity scope (as above), quorum semantics when the population is transient (does `min_endorsements: 3` mean anything when four agents are active vs forty?), and whether suggested-class pins rot. Fixture-04 is scoped in the conformance README to cover the whole civic layer when we get there.
